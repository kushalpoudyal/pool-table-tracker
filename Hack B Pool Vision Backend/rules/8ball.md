# House rules: eight-ball (International rules)

The observer reads this file on every look. Edit it to change the rules; no code changes needed.

## The balls

Every ball has its own number and colour. As soon as a number can be read on a ball, the number
decides which ball it is. The colour then confirms it, and so does solid vs stripe.

| Number | Colour | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| cue (no number) | white | cue ball |
| 1 | yellow | solid |
| 2 | blue | solid |
| 3 | red | solid |
| 4 | purple | solid |
| 5 | orange | solid |
| 6 | green | solid |
| 7 | maroon (brown-red) | solid |
| 8 | black | solid colour, but it is the 8-ball, in neither group |
| 9 | yellow | stripe (white ball with a yellow band) |
| 10 | blue | stripe |
| 11 | red | stripe |
| 12 | purple | stripe |
| 13 | orange | stripe |
| 14 | green | stripe |
| 15 | maroon | stripe |

- Each colour except black and white appears twice: once solid (1-7), once striped (9-15).
- A stripe seen from above can show mostly its white cap, so don't mistake it for the cue ball. The
  cue ball has no colour at all.
- There is exactly one cue ball and one 8-ball, at most 7 solids and at most 7 stripes.

## The game (International eight-ball rules, as played in Australia; the groups are solids and stripes)

- 15 object balls: solids 1-7, stripes 9-15, and the black 8. Plus the white cue ball.
- The break is the first shot. The table stays open after the break, whatever is potted: balls potted
  on the break never decide the groups. An 8 potted on the break is put back on the table, so don't
  list it as potted; it is not a win or a loss.
- Open table: the first player to pot a ball legally after the break takes that group (solids or
  stripes), the other player the other group. If both groups go in on that shot, the group the cue
  ball hit first decides.
- A player's visit goes on while they legally pot a ball of their own group. Otherwise the turn
  passes. Potting only the other group's balls after hitting their own first ends the visit; it is
  not a foul.
- Fouls. The other player gets one visit with the cue ball in hand (after a scratch on the break,
  from behind the baulk line):
  - the cue ball is potted (a scratch) or leaves the table;
  - the cue ball hits no ball, or first hits a ball that is not the shooter's (on an open table,
    hitting the 8 first is a foul);
  - after the cue ball hits a ball, nothing is potted and no ball reaches a cushion.
- Balls potted on a foul stay down.
- The 8 is a player's ball only once their whole group was potted on earlier shots. Potting it then,
  without a foul, wins.
- Apart from the break, a player loses if they pot the 8 while any of their group is still on the
  table, on the same shot as their last group ball, or with a foul (e.g. scratching).
- No called pockets.
